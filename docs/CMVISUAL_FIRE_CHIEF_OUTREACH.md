# CMVisual — Fire Chief Outreach Package

## Recommended first email

**Subject: Local pilot idea for firefighter spatial awareness — CMVisual**

Chief Schneider,

I’m developing CMVisual, an open spatial-awareness system designed to combine actual camera and validated sensor observations into one live spatial picture for emergency response.

I’m not asking Lebanon Fire Department to buy a finished product. I’m looking for a local fire/rescue partner willing to review the concept and determine whether a small, controlled training pilot could improve situational awareness, sensor coordination and incident replay.

The system is built around a strict rule: measured information is separated from derived or predicted information, and it reports insufficient data rather than inventing an answer.

Would you be open to a short demonstration and technical discussion?

— CMVisual

## 20-second phone opener

“Chief, I’m developing a spatial-awareness system called CMVisual for emergency response. It combines real camera and validated sensor observations into one live spatial picture and keeps track of where the information came from and how certain it is. I’m not calling to sell you anything today. I’m looking for a local department willing to look at the technology and tell me whether a controlled training pilot would actually be useful to firefighters. Could I show you what I’m building?”

## If the Chief asks: “What exactly does it do?”

“Think of it as a spatial evidence layer over the incident scene. It can take real observations from cameras and validated external sensors, place them into a common spatial view, track them over time, show uncertainty and source quality, and preserve the observations for replay. It does not claim to see through walls unless an actual validated sensor provides that measurement.”

## If the Chief asks: “Why would firefighters need it?”

“The problem we’re targeting is incomplete situational awareness. Smoke, darkness, unfamiliar structures, changing hazards and multiple teams make it difficult to build a mental picture from separate information sources. CMVisual is intended to make the verified information spatially understandable without pretending the system knows something it cannot measure.”

## If the Chief asks: “What do you need from us?”

“Initially, very little. I need experienced responders to identify the information that actually matters, help define a safe training scenario and evaluate whether the system improves understanding. The first stage should be a demonstration and controlled evaluation, not a purchase.”

## If the Chief asks: “How much does it cost?”

“For the first conversation, I’m not proposing a purchase price. I want to establish whether there is a real operational problem worth solving and define a measurable pilot. If the pilot proves useful, we can discuss the appropriate deployment and funding path.”

## If the Chief asks about funding

“Because CMVisual is a technology R&D project, I’m also pursuing non-dilutive R&D funding. NSF’s current SBIR/STTR solicitation, NSF 26-510, has a November 4, 2026 full-proposal deadline and offers Phase I awards up to $305,000. A fire/rescue validation partner could help establish the real-world problem and test requirements, subject to program eligibility and award rules.”
