# CMVisual — Lebanon Fire Department Pilot Proposal

## Working title
**CMVisual: Evidence-Based Spatial Awareness for Fire & Rescue Operations**

## Prepared for
Lebanon Fire Department — Lebanon, Missouri

The Lebanon Fire Department is a full-time career department serving the city and responding to fire, medical, technical rescue, hazardous-materials and disaster incidents, including through automatic and mutual aid. The proposed pilot is intentionally designed around those operational realities.

## Executive proposition

Firefighters frequently operate with incomplete spatial information: smoke, darkness, unfamiliar structures, changing hazards, fragmented sensor feeds and multiple teams moving at once.

**CMVisual is being developed to turn validated sensor observations into one understandable spatial operating picture.**

The objective is not to replace firefighter judgment. The objective is to provide firefighters and command personnel with better, more traceable information when conditions make ordinary visual awareness difficult.

## What CMVisual is designed to do

- Combine camera observations with validated external sensor measurements.
- Display observations spatially on a live map, floor-plan or 3D representation.
- Maintain stable observation tracks when supported by real sensor data.
- Show source, timestamp, confidence, uncertainty and sensor quality.
- Corroborate observations from multiple authorized devices when coordinate alignment is established.
- Preserve a time-based evidence record for later replay and review.
- Forecast physical movement or spatial outcomes from repeated measurements without presenting forecasts as facts.
- Clearly separate measured, derived, corroborated, predicted and unresolved information.
- Remain useful when the available sensors cannot answer a question by explicitly reporting insufficient data.

## What CMVisual does NOT claim

CMVisual will not fabricate people, room geometry, sensor readings, biometrics or through-wall observations.

A camera detection is identified as a camera observation. A nonvisual observation is displayed only when supplied by a validated external sensor. Ordinary phone Wi-Fi, cellular, microphone or motion sensors are not treated as proof of a person behind a wall.

This evidence discipline is a core product requirement because emergency-response software must not turn uncertainty into false certainty.

## Proposed pilot

### Phase 1 — operational discovery
30–60 minute conversation with command/training personnel.

Goals:
- Identify the highest-value firefighter information gaps.
- Identify existing sensors and data sources.
- Define a controlled test environment.
- Select 3–5 measurable success criteria.

### Phase 2 — controlled demonstration
Use a safe training environment rather than a live emergency.

Example measurements:
- observation localization accuracy
- update latency
- sensor agreement/disagreement
- time required to understand a scene
- operator workload/usability
- usefulness of replay and evidence history

### Phase 3 — evaluation
Compare CMVisual-assisted performance with the department's existing workflow. The goal is evidence, not a predetermined positive result.

## Proposed success criteria

A pilot should be considered successful only if the department can document measurable improvement in at least some of these areas:

1. Faster spatial understanding.
2. Better awareness of verified observations.
3. Clearer distinction between known and unknown conditions.
4. Useful multi-device spatial coordination.
5. Useful incident replay.
6. Acceptable latency and reliability.
7. High operator comprehension under realistic workload.

## Why Lebanon

Lebanon is an unusually practical environment for an initial validation program because the community combines a professional fire/rescue organization with substantial manufacturing, engineering and industrial activity. The city's official economic-development information identifies major employers including White River Marine Group, Copeland, Missouri Cooperage, The Durham Company, G3 Boats, MEP, DTMP and DT Engineering.

That creates a potential local validation path from emergency response into industrial safety, inspection, automation and spatial operations after the first fire/rescue evaluation.

## The request

**We are not asking Lebanon Fire Department to purchase an unproven finished product.**

We are asking for an opportunity to demonstrate the system, learn from experienced responders and determine whether a controlled pilot can produce useful measurable evidence.

If the technology proves useful, the department could become an early validation partner as CMVisual develops toward a deployable emergency-response system.

## Closing

Firefighters already have valuable information sources. The challenge is making that information spatially understandable, synchronized and trustworthy when the environment is changing faster than a person can interpret separate screens.

**CMVisual is being built to close that gap.**

### The goal is simple:
**Give responders the clearest trustworthy spatial information available when it matters most.**
