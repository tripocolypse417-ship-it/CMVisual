# CMVisual Client Sales Pack — Lebanon Fire & Rescue

## 1. Primary objective
Secure a discovery/demo conversation and a controlled validation pilot — not an immediate software purchase.

## 2. Client-specific positioning
Lebanon Fire Department is a full-time career department serving the city and responding to fire suppression, emergency medical, technical rescue, hazardous materials, disaster management, and fire prevention needs. It also participates in automatic and mutual aid. The CMVisual pitch therefore emphasizes shared situational awareness, sensor evidence, uncertainty, incident replay, and controlled validation rather than generic AI claims.

## 3. Chief-ready email
Subject: CMVisual — local firefighter situational-awareness pilot

Chief Schneider,

I am developing CMVisual, an open spatial-evidence platform designed to combine actual camera and validated sensor observations into one operational spatial picture.

I believe the technology may have a practical application in fire/rescue: helping crews and command personnel understand where verified observations are occurring, which sensors produced them, how fresh and reliable the information is, and how the situation changes over time.

I am not asking the department to purchase an unproven product. I am asking for a short demonstration and discussion about a controlled training/validation scenario where firefighters could determine whether the information is actually useful.

CMVisual is deliberately evidence-first: measured information is separated from derived and predicted information, and the system reports insufficient data rather than inventing an answer.

If you are willing to review it, I would appreciate 20 minutes to demonstrate the concept and hear what your crews would actually need.

Joshua Marks
CMVisual

I’m looking for a local fire department to evaluate the concept in a controlled training scenario—not to buy anything today. I’d like to show you what I have and get your operational feedback.”

## 5. Meeting agenda
1. Five-minute problem discussion: where crews lose spatial information.
2. Five-minute CMVisual demonstration.
3. Five-minute controlled pilot proposal.
4. Five-minute questions, constraints, and next step.

## 6. Pilot offer
Phase 1 — discovery: identify one training scenario and the information firefighters need.
Phase 2 — controlled demonstration: use only approved real observations/sensors.
Phase 3 — measurement: compare defined tasks before and after CMVisual use.
Phase 4 — review: determine what worked, failed, or needs engineering changes.

No claim of life-saving performance should be made until validated. The pilot should measure useful operational outcomes such as time to establish spatial understanding, observation localization accuracy, operator workload, sensor disagreement handling, and replay usefulness.

## 7. Objection handling
**“Does it see through walls?”**
Only when an appropriate validated nonvisual sensor supplies an observation. CMVisual does not manufacture through-wall people or structures from ordinary phone sensors.

**“Is this AI guessing?”**
The architecture explicitly separates measured, derived, corroborated, predicted, unresolved, and insufficient-data states.

**“Do we need special equipment?”**
The platform is designed to integrate available validated sensors. The pilot should start with equipment the department can safely and legally use.

**“What does it cost?”**
The first conversation is a validation discussion. Commercial pricing should follow demonstrated value, deployment scope, support requirements, and sensor integration requirements.

**“Why should we be involved this early?”**
Because firefighters can determine operational usefulness much better than a software developer working alone. The department would help define requirements and acceptance tests before the product is finalized.

## 8. Close
“Would you be open to a short demonstration and a discussion of one controlled training scenario? If the technology does not produce measurable value for your crews, we should know that early. If it does, we can build the next version around evidence from the people who actually use it.”

After fire/rescue validation, target Lebanon industrial organizations with spatial safety, automation, inspection, or facility-awareness use cases. Lebanon's official major-employer list includes White River Marine Group, Copeland, Missouri Cooperage, The Durham Company, G3 Boats, Lowe Boats, Marine Electrical Products, DTMP, Marathon, and DT Engineering.

Priority industrial message: “CMVisual turns distributed sensor observations into an evidence-backed spatial operating picture for people, equipment, facilities, and incidents.”

## 11. Funding message
Do not pitch a grant as guaranteed funding. Position the pilot as customer validation that strengthens future R&D funding applications. NSF SBIR/STTR NSF 26-510 is a current route worth evaluating; the solicitation lists Phase I awards up to $305,000 and a November 4, 2026 full-proposal deadline. Eligibility and the Project Pitch process must be confirmed before submission.

## 12. Non-negotiable credibility rules
- Never fabricate sensor observations.
- Never claim unsupported through-wall human detection.
- Never infer identity, intent, hostility, dangerousness, mental state, personality, or neural state from appearance, gait, motion, proximity, or ordinary radio/camera signals.
- Preserve source, time, location, uncertainty, confidence, and validation status.
- Use controlled demonstrations and measurable acceptance criteria.
- Never represent a projection or prediction as a measured fact.
